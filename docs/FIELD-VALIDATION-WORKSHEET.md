## Fifteen-minute conversation

First five minutes: ask how the person currently records observations, associates photographs, finds related records and handles poor connectivity. Ask which information must remain private and what would prevent adoption.

Next five minutes: show one task—save a note, find a related record, inspect the source and choose its sharing policy. Let them explain what they expect before helping them. Record difficulty and comments, not just praise.

Final five minutes: ask for the single most useful change, the most serious risk, and a suitable small reference dataset. Request permission for any material they offer; do not request unpublished excavation locations or restricted records for a public demo.

## Video outline

1. Explain the field-memory problem as a hypothesis.

4. Show the privacy choice and a real exchange.
5. Explain one improvement made from feedback.

Do not imply museum/ASI sponsorship, expert authentication of objects, measured time savings or deployment unless those actually occurred. If no interview is available, use a clearly labelled team usability test and say so.

## Dataset choices for the conversation

Use the synthetic expedition for repeatable synchronization/conflict demonstrations. Use the eight-record museum pack for sourced retrieval. They represent different regions and periods and should not be presented as one excavation. A domain expert should select a narrower pack for a field pilot.

Bring a permitted ordinary-object photograph to demonstrate camera/upload mechanics if no archaeological image is available; label it as a demonstration object.
